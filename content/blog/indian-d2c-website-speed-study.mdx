India's D2C boom is mobile-first. The unpaid question sitting under every ad rupee and every influencer reel is quieter: **when someone opens your brand site on a budget Android phone, does the page actually show up in time to shop?**

Bellops ran a reproducible lab speed study on the public homepages of **50 well-known Indian D2C brands** — beauty, fashion, food, home, electronics, wellness, eyewear, jewellery, and baby — under a **Lighthouse mobile Slow 4G** profile meant to stress a roughly **₹10,000-class** phone experience. **49 brands** produced a valid LCP and enter the ranking; **Yoga Bar** is listed but excluded from ranks (see methodology). This article is the published home for that work: methodology, rankings, patterns, and practical fixes.

### Why speed on budget phones matters in India

India's internet is overwhelmingly wireless. TRAI's *Indian Telecom Services Performance Indicators* for the year ending **31 March 2025** reported about **969.10 million** internet subscribers, of which roughly **927.70 million** were wireless internet subscribers, with average wireless data usage around **21.53 GB** per subscriber per month ([TRAI yearly indicators PDF](https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-07/YIR_08072025.pdf)).

Smartphones remain the front door. IDC reported India smartphone shipments of about **151 million** units in **2024**, with mass-budget 5G devices still a large share of the mix even as average selling prices rose ([IDC India 2024 summary](https://my.idc.com/getdoc.jsp?containerId=prAP53185725)). Premium demand has grown — but a large volume of shopping still happens on mid and budget Android devices, not on flagship launch-day hardware.

Speed is not a vanity metric. Google's Core Web Vitals guidance asks site owners to aim for **LCP under 2.5s**, **INP under 200ms**, and **CLS under 0.1** for a "good" experience ([Google Search Central — Core Web Vitals](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/core-web-vitals)). Separately, Deloitte's *Milliseconds Make Millions* analysis (Europe/US brand mobile data) associated a **0.1s** mobile speed improvement with roughly **8.4%** higher retail conversions and **9.2%** higher average order value ([Deloitte overview](https://www.deloitte.com/ie/en/services/consulting/research/milliseconds-make-millions.html)). That study is not India-specific — treat it as directional evidence that small latency wins compound in retail, not as an India conversion guarantee.

For D2C brands spending heavily on Meta, Google, and creator ads, a slow homepage is a leak: paid traffic lands, the hero image stalls, the "Add to cart" path is not yet interactive, and the session ends. Budget phones amplify every unused kilobyte, every main-thread script, and every layout shift.

### What we tested (methodology summary)

- **Brands:** 50 Indian D2C storefronts across category groups (beauty 10, fashion 10, food 10 attempted / 9 ranked, home 5, electronics 4, health 6, eyewear 1, jewellery 2, baby 2).
- **Tool:** Lighthouse **12.8.2**, lab mobile emulation, **simulated Slow 4G**.
- **Aggregation:** **Median of 3** successful homepage runs per brand.
- **Primary sort:** median homepage **LCP** ascending (ties would break on TBT, then CLS, then Performance score — none needed at the top).
- **Pages:** Homepage only this pass (PDPs deferred).
- **Window:** **27 Oct 2026, 13:13–14:30 IST** (Asia/Calcutta).
- **Vantage:** Lab runs from a **Toronto** host. Prefer relative ranks over absolute times when comparing to India field experience.
- **Field data:** CrUX LCP/INP/CLS **not collected** this pass (PSI API daily quota exceeded). We will add field medians later — do not treat lab LCP as Google ranking data.
- **Follow-ups applied before freeze:** CaratLane and FreshToHome re-run pinned to **India** storefronts; CaratLane and BlueStone categorised as **jewellery** (not eyewear); Lenskart still geo-routed to `/en-us` (footnote).
- **Excluded from ranking:** Yoga Bar — Lighthouse reported **NO_LCP** (page loaded; no valid LCP element under the lab profile).

### Patterns visible in the medians (not a full opportunity audit)

We did not freeze a Lighthouse "opportunities" audit sheet for every URL in this package. These counts are **derived from the published medians** and should be read as triage signals, not root-cause certificates:

1. **Page weight pressure** — **27 of 49** ranked sites transferred more than ~5 MB (median); **11** exceeded ~10 MB. Heavy heroes, carousels, and uncompressed assets are the usual suspects.
2. **Main-thread / TBT pressure** — **33 of 49** had median TBT above 1,000 ms; **4** above 5,000 ms (including Dot & Key's extreme outlier and several campaign-heavy beauty/wellness storefronts).
3. **Request sprawl** — **26 of 49** median request counts exceeded 400.
4. **Layout instability** — **10 of 49** had median CLS ≥ 0.1 (Google's "good" CLS threshold).
5. **Nobody cleared the lab LCP bar** — **0 of 49** hit ≤ 2.5s under this Slow 4G + Toronto lab profile. That is a harsh lab bar; field CrUX (pending) will often look better — but the relative gap between brands still matters for paid-traffic quality.

### What the faster sites tend to do differently

Without claiming each top brand's private architecture, the patterns that usually separate fast D2C storefronts from heavy ones are consistent:

1. **Hero restraint.** One carefully compressed LCP image (or a lightweight treatment), correctly sized for mobile, in AVIF/WebP with a sensible `srcset`. No autoplay background video above the fold unless it is truly brand-critical and budgeted.
2. **HTML that paints early.** Critical content in the first response — not locked behind a large client bundle.
3. **Script diet.** Consent-aware tags; defer non-essential chat, reviews, and personalisation until after interaction or idle.
4. **Stable layout.** Reserved space for images, banners, and sticky bars.
5. **Edge delivery.** CDN close to Indian users, strong cache hit ratios, compressed text, HTTP/2 or HTTP/3.
6. **Performance budgets in CI.** A maximum KB and LCP target on every release.
7. **Real-device empathy.** Flagship MacBooks lie. Budget Android + mid-tier 4G is closer to many Indian shopping sessions.

### Actionable fixes (prioritised)

**This week (often high ROI)**  
- Compress and properly dimension the LCP image; set `fetchpriority="high"` on it; preload sparingly.  
- Defer non-critical JS; remove dead tags; split the chat widget behind a click.  
- Fix CLS: width/height or aspect-ratio boxes; avoid inserting top banners without reserved space.  
- Enable modern image formats and CDN caching for static paths.

**This month**  
- Move above-the-fold rendering server-side for homepage and PDP.  
- Introduce a performance budget and stick to it through campaign season.  
- Audit third parties quarterly; require owners for every script.  
- Test on a real ~₹10k Android device on a non-Wi‑Fi network (and preferably from an India network).

**This quarter**  
- Rebuild heavy theme sections that force mega-bundles.  
- Align design-system components with performance (carousels, 3D, animation) — premium visuals can still be fast when streamed and gated.  
- Connect CrUX + RUM dashboards to release reviews.  
- If you invest in immersive or 3D experiences, plan them as progressive enhancement: fast base commerce first, richer layers on capable devices and after first paint. That is the philosophy behind thoughtful [premium website design](/premium-website-design) and [3D website design](/3d-website-design) — not decoration that blocks the cart. [SEO services](/seo-services) and conversion-focused [landing pages](/landing-pages) only pay off if the destination actually paints.

### How to read this study fairly

A slow lab score is not a verdict on product quality, brand love, or offline retail strength. Many teams ship under intense campaign calendars. The constructive reading is: **these numbers show where engineering attention pays back media spend.** If your brand appears in the opportunity list, treat it as a backlog input. If you appear near the top, protect the budget that got you there.

### FAQ

**What did this Bellops study measure?**  
Lab mobile performance (LCP, TBT, CLS, Performance score, transfer size, request count) for 50 Indian D2C homepages. 49 brands ranked by median LCP; Yoga Bar excluded for NO_LCP. CrUX field metrics were not available this pass and will be added later.

**Does a ₹10,000 phone really change the results?**  
Low-end CPU and Slow 4G network profiles expose main-thread and weight issues that flagship devices hide. This pass used Lighthouse mobile simulation (not a named physical handset). Absolute times are further affected by the Toronto lab vantage.

**Is lab data the same as what Google uses for ranking?**  
No. Google uses real-user (field) experience among many factors. Lab data is for diagnosis; CrUX is closer to field reality when available. We do not yet have CrUX for this cohort.

**Why are some famous brands missing or footnoted?**  
Dead domains, non-D2C primary models, or unresolved storefronts were replaced during list building. Lenskart is included but measured on `/en-us`. Yoga Bar is listed but unranked (NO_LCP). Bewakoof and Wakefit returned curl 403 from datacenter IPs but succeeded under Chrome/Lighthouse and are ranked.

**Will you name the slowest brands to shame them?**  
No. We publish numbers constructively and welcome right-of-reply / addenda after meaningful fixes.

**How can a D2C team improve LCP quickly?**  
Optimise the true LCP element (usually the hero image), reduce competing above-the-fold requests, and defer non-critical scripts.

**Do 3D or premium visuals always hurt speed?**  
Not if they are progressively enhanced, lazy-loaded, and kept off the critical path. Fast base experience first.
